# TradePress Blog Post - Interview Questions

## Project Origin & Motivation

1. **What was the specific problem or gap you identified that led you to create TradePress?** Was there an existing tool or workflow that frustrated you, or did you see an opportunity no one else was addressing?
As a new investor, who tried every platform and app that I could find, my overall feeling was that most interfaces are created by similar minded people. While it may be true that a massive amount of users
are also like minded - my gut is telling me that there's room for someone who thinks and designs out of the box - a little innovation. 

I believe there are aspects missing within the entire industry. That may be changing fast with the introduction of AI but so far everything suggests that AI is going to provide something similar to what already
exists, just faster in terms of analysis, and packaged to appear like an easy route to trading success in some cases. 

My intention is to not only offer adaptive interfaces but offer entirely difference experiences for different levels of users. I feel so strongly about the need to bring more people onto the stock market
that I want to create the pathway that doesn't exist for people who need an entirely different approach. 

I'm going to be careful in how much I reveal because after a year of development, there's still a lot of confidence that something good is going to come for this. I fully intend to use TradePress for my
own trading needs and so I'm designing it for complete success. That may begin as a support tool but will hopefully do a lot more in the future i.e. automatic trading with no costs to get started and be
giving access to sound information.

The gap I want to fill is one for people who usually lose their money through retail accounts because the path to trading is not suitable for everyone. 
I have principles and methodologies and theories in how we do that properly, being bold enough to claim it either hasn't been or any attempt to do so isn't extensive enough that I've seen so far. 

2. **When did you first conceive the idea, and what was the "aha moment"?** Was it a gradual realization or a sudden insight?
The idea came about gradually as I was learning to trade and investing time in understanding the market. The "aha moment" occurred when I realized that most trading platforms were designed with a one-size-fits-all approach, which didn't cater to the diverse needs of traders. I saw an opportunity to create a platform that could adapt to different trading styles and levels of expertise, providing a more personalized experience.

I dived into trading, trying both long term investing tools and day-trading strategies. My self determined approach was to expose myself to practicel and pyschological experiences. I often reffered to my experiences
being part of an education and potential loses being the cost of education - just as educational does have a cost and nobody expects a return from their college or university. 

My biggest realisation was the noise generated by people with an agenda - there is a weekly cycle of trying to lure inexperienced traders into making reactive decisions. 
I wanted a tool that clears up the noise and helps to manage both portfolio and high risk CFD trading during busy days. 

3. **Who is your primary user?** Are you building this for retail traders, professional analysts, or a specific niche within the trading community?

It could be section of retail traders - those who feel most out of their depth and need a more mindful guidance into the stock market. 
Professionals usually have the time to use and take highly successful approaches, their strategies well tested and suitable for those who are able to make a living by focusing on a strategy. 

I suppose in a sense it's a niche set of tools aimed at a niche audience - but that isn't to say I'm not aiming for an intelligent tool that offers 70% success rate. That will always be the
goal and even if that's based on an informative tool rather than automated trading. I will want the information to reflect good signals.

## Technical Architecture Decisions

4. **Why WordPress and PHP specifically?** What advantages did you see compared to building a standalone application or using other frameworks (Node.js, Python, etc.)?
WordPress and PHP were chosen for several reasons. First, WordPress offers a robust and flexible platform that allows for rapid development and deployment. This is going to be key to rapidly insert into a saturated market of automated, semi-automated, many approaches to signals and bots, and the highly popular platforms such as TradingView which will always be invaluable resources. I want my tools to compliment the use of other tools, not compete or make the bolds claims that many platforms don't hold back on expressing, to make their own profit.

WordPress offers an extensive plugin ecosystem provides a wealth of pre-built functionality that can be leveraged to accelerate development. This may be useful at the stage of creating my own website and community for trading. One that aims to focus on my Scoring Directives and algorithmns. I need fast prototyping and delivery of concepts to generate early interest - WordPress is key to doing that.

PHP is a widely used server-side scripting language with a large community and extensive documentation. This makes it easier to find resources and support during development. Additionally, PHP's compatibility with various databases and hosting environments ensures flexibility in deployment options. It has improved extensively, as has WordPress effeciency, and the web technology to quickly create UI concepts that can be instantly delivered for testing/feedback. 

5. **How does WordPress's plugin ecosystem benefit TradePress?** Are you leveraging existing WordPress features, or is it more about distribution and accessibility?
The ability to choose plugins that add additional communication tools, social network sharing, management of data and presenting that data is unique compared to platforms that act as services, not solutions to be adapted and suit different minds. 

I especially tend to think, work, and visualise things differently. An ecosystem of plugins can allow the trialing of any setup that comes to the imagination, without the costs of developing entirely new apps/websites.

6. **What role does the multi-platform API integration play in your vision?** Why support 20+ platforms instead of focusing on one or two?
The multi-platform API integration is crucial to TradePress's vision of being a versatile and comprehensive trading tool. By supporting 20+ platforms, TradePress can cater to a broader audience with diverse trading preferences and needs. Different traders use different platforms based on their strategies, asset classes, and personal preferences. 

This is a goal. Early development focuses on the lowest costing platforms to reduce development costs. These are also the platform that new traders are likely to be more interested in. 
The plugin has a system for automatically switching between platforms and will take advantage of limit rates by taking overall allowance into consideration over the trading period. This is mostly in effect but the trading period is set to 24 hours - the ability to tell TradePress to balance rate limiting over a specified number of hours i.e. 5 hours of intended trading, will allow a more intense frequency of updating. 


7. **The "Scoring Directives" concept seems central to TradePress. Can you explain the thinking behind this architecture?** How does it differ from traditional indicator-based systems?

The "Scoring Directives" concept is designed to provide a more holistic and adaptive approach to trading signals compared to traditional indicator-based systems. Traditional systems often rely on fixed indicators that may not account for the dynamic nature of the market or the individual trader's strategy. I vision a UI that works well with touch screens - the next generation of traders have tablets and ipads. I also wanted something that cuts through the noise and can be configured to suit different strategies. The scoring system combines a set of rules to order stocks to indicate priority in comparison to other stocks the trade is interested in - with potential features for randomly suggested stocks that offer high confidence trades within the parameters of the strategy. As someone with limited money, I wanted to maximise the betting in CFD, opening and closing quickly. My intention is for the fast changing, dynamic scoring system, to not only suggest when a stock is prime for opening a position in but also when it's moment for taking profit comes.


Additional to the high risk trading support is to be optional safegaurds, warnings and potentally automatic trading actions to help protect new traders.

I found that it was difficult to take advantage of opportunities during busy days, even moderatetly busy days. I once made the newbie mistake of leaving a CFD position without stop-loss when a neighbour chapped my door and asked for help with their TV. My approach was to actively monitor and close positions in that particular series of trading, which worked for me for weeks, but resulted in a loss of £500 after a profit of £1000. 
I essentially halved a big win due to a lack of experience and allowing myself to be distracted. I consider these moments as education and turning the experiences into not just trading tools, but educational, and intelligent guidance, covering emotional and psychology, factoring the possibilities of these factoring into users actions and what the results of trades might cause.

## Core Features & Prototyping

8. **Which features are currently working prototypes, and which are you most proud of?** What took the longest to get right?
The UI that dynamically changes the order a list of stocks was simply to prototype as a web page, the algorithm behind it has to be complex and consumes many PHP classes. 
The real-time data fetching and intelligent caching system was one of the most challenging features to implement. It required careful consideration of API rate limits and data freshness to ensure that users receive accurate and timely information without exceeding usage quotas. The user has to trust that scores are based on the complete process configured in a strategy

9. **The strategy builder with weighted directives—how did you arrive at this approach?** Did you test other methodologies first?
I have a strong need for control over my tools in order to trust them and be satisfied over how they operated because in my eyes there's an improvement to be made in everything. 
The weighted approach tells me that my own understanding of indicators applied to stock also matches the results of the scoring algorithmn, creating more trust in myself and the plugin. 
Despite how complex and ambitious the approach to weighting is, there was a drive to deliver something that people find acceptable. I felt that in order to do that, the entire system
has to be configurable because the importance of any aspect of a stocks information is debatable, especially within different strategies, and so weights were made configurable. 

I'm still learning, and felt the tool needs to be able to adapt to my understanding of how each possible effect will change a stock price in relation to other applied effects. 
Some of those effects are temporary, such as political elections, and some happen periodically throughout the year, so I considered importance being reflected in a dynamic weight. 

I often vision a more interactive, enjoyable approach or the need for more adaptiveness in interfaces. Once I get ideas in my head they're there forever. 



10. **Real-time data with intelligent caching is mentioned. What challenges did you face with API rate limiting and data freshness?**
Testing with a strict limit was difficult at first but I quickly got started on a cache system that recognises market data has a mix of shelf lives.
I quickly realised that caching has to be global and not per feature, to reduce additional API calls, even if the use for the data is very different. 
It's also important to recognise that a data object/array may include data that has a longer life than other values, and so extracting those became important. 
This is an example of where I use WordPress core caching as the quick, bulk storage approach, but also insert to custom database tables for the longterm record keeping of a stock. 
There's some ambition to store history within the schema design but I've yet to determine how far to go on that. 
Additionally, the expiry and refresh of data, especially that which changes many times per hour, would ideally be processed outside of algorithmns, reducing the intensity and combined demand by a single request. 

I've no access to information on how large platforms handle data but I'm constantly wondering. I vision an approach where there is a set number of seconds for multiple groups i.e. 3600, 60, 10, 1. Then different values are assigned to each of those, a system executing the assigned API queries as time passes. The problem with this, is rate limits for the user who can't afford to pay for the potential demand that might create, and so the plugin needs to support strategies that not only work, but they can work using long life caches.

## Future Vision & Roadmap

11. **Looking at your Phase 2 and Phase 3 roadmap, what excites you most?** Is it automated trading execution, ML optimization, or something else?
I'm actually excited about applying a Trailing Stop Loss to portfolio. If a user is using a platform that doesn't have an API then TradePress will alert them when
their portfolio has reached a certain profit level, and then suggest applying an imitated Trailing Stop Loss to protect profits, and that stop loss will only alert them. 
In that scenario the user will be responsible for reacting, so not strictly a "Stop Loss" and it would need to be expressed as something else in that sense but it's the same
idea of trying not to be caught out when we're not monitoring our investments hourly - something only professionals do really. 

12. **You mention a "community strategy marketplace" in Phase 3. What would that look like, and how would it differentiate TradePress?**
The community strategy marketplace would be a platform where users can share, buy, and sell trading strategies they've developed using TradePress. This would foster a collaborative environment where traders of all levels can benefit from each other's insights and experiences. I'm aware that there may eventuall be some favorites and the marketplace becomes obselete but if the collaboration and sharing helps drive the plugins use and fine-tunes it's use, it will be worth it. Technically it will be the sharing of a file or transfer of data, not really a complex system at all. 

What I would be keen to do is find a way to run submitted strategies - potentially on a set of historical data, maybe more than one set, then using the results to sort of score/rate a strategy. Doing this as a standard gauge might help to encourage high performing strategies, maybe even a competative community. 

13. **Are there any features you're deliberately *not* building, or ideas you've rejected? Why?**
I have little experience with options so that hasn't been factored in.

## Personal Perspective

17. **What's your background in trading and software development?** How do these two worlds inform your design decisions?
I have a background in software development with experience in web technologies, particularly WordPress and PHP. My journey into trading began as a personal endeavor to understand the stock market and improve my financial literacy. This dual perspective allows me to approach TradePress with a unique blend of technical expertise and practical trading experience.

My software development skills enable me to design and implement complex features, while my trading experience informs the user-centric design decisions that prioritize usability and effectiveness. I understand the challenges traders face, especially beginners, and I aim to create a platform that addresses those pain points through intuitive design and robust functionality.

18. **What's the biggest challenge you've faced building TradePress so far?** Technical, time management, or something else?
Initially, I struggled with constant chicken-egg scenarios, with demo data being used to establish interfaces, then losing track of which features were displaying demo data and which were live. I had added the ability to switch to a demo a mode to assist development and testing. Automation and background processing was an additional complexity that needed the UI to make progress while the code did. 

It got to a stage, where I was spending as much time on documentation, to manage the project, as I was coding. I needed constant reminding of the technicals that I had decided were priority and the specific order that I had placed them in to avoid finding myself in a mess. 

Documentation was being added to constantly during a period of brain storming and planned tasks became integrated into the plugin itself. There is an admin page for viewing the roadmap. 
That period of brain storming led to lots of little prototypes to help me visualise different interfaces and determine which ones would be needed to test functions, and PHP classes outside of the more complex, and automated system. This involved constant decision making, identifying problems and solutions, many times per hour. 

 I also felt that I needed some views simply to feel re-assured that data was being imported and updated correctly, and that it was easier to do that with more UI output than trying to generate vague logs, which I do across around 10 different log files haha! Different forms of data and general information are like individual elements with potentially different sources of data i.e. general news, the market calendar, watchlists, and company information. I could see a potential connection between each view and the intention to inform the user of opportunities within all interfaces - but that was exploratory. Once I got into that mindset of an extensive trading suit I found myself down a rabbit hole, it took many weeks to finish a flurry of ideas, around 50 different none-functionality admin views because I felt the need to visualise the entire scope of the plugin and fully grasp the priority for all my ideas.
