## The gap, stated plainly

> A contested position that the StopGuardian now **trails** is still **not time-stopped**.

So the position is protected on one axis (stop distance) and unmanaged on the other (time). That is a **half-governed position**, and it is the exact shape R31 ruled against — *"managed, by its strictest claimant"*.

## Why it was not folded into #1041

It is a **second behaviour change on the same ruling**. #1041's guard is canaried for the guardian path only; bolting a second service onto it would give one canary responsibility for two independent behaviours, so a failure would not say which. It gets its own decision, its own marker, and its own both-direction canary.

## Scope

1. Decide whether the time stop takes the same **strictest claimant** as the guardian, or a different rule (the time stop is a *duration*, and the strictest claimant owns the *stop distance* — those need not be the same bot, and **the ruling does not say**).
2. If the same claimant: resolve it through the same source of truth so the two services cannot drift apart. ⚠️ A second independent implementation of "who owns this position" is the drift the repo warns about most.
3. Guard whichever rule is chosen, **both directions** — canary it by planting the pre-change behaviour and confirming the test fails.
4. Record it in all three homes (`DECISION` marker + `docs/SETTLED-DECISIONS.md` + this issue), citing row 59.

## ⚠️ Do not assume the answer

The time stop's refusal may be **correct**. A time stop closes a position outright; a guardian adjustment does not. Closing a position that several bots believe they own may be a bigger act than nudging its stop, and "several bots disagree" is a reasonable reason for the *closing* service to decline while the *adjusting* service proceeds. **Measure and decide — do not inherit #1041's answer by analogy.**
