## Summary

The bar-timestamp invariants are all **time-based**, and a time-based bound **cannot** detect the
defect that #832 fixed — because a legitimate next-bar publish and a spurious duplicate-bucket row
are both simply "ahead of now by less than one cadence".

The real discriminator is **duplication**, not timing. Nothing checks for it.

## The evidence

`INV-NO-FUTURE-ROWS` allows a lead of `cadence × 2`:

| timeframe | permitted lead |
|---|---|
| `1m` | 2 min |
| `15m` | 30 min |
| `4h` | **8 hours** |

The #832 defect was a `4h` row **52 minutes** ahead. **Comfortably inside the allowance** — so the
invariant passed while **29 future-stamped rows sat in the table**, and one bot could not trade.

Meanwhile the writer guard added by #832 allows **2 minutes**. So the two now disagree by up to
**240×**, and the invariant is protection in name only:

```
writer guard (isBarFutureStamped) :  2 minutes
invariant   (LEAD_FOR)            :  up to 8 hours (4h)
```

## Why tightening the time bound is not the fix

Even `1 × cadence` fails to separate the two cases on a slow timeframe:

- **Legitimate**: a provider publishes the just-opened next bar → ahead by up to one cadence
- **#832 defect**: a duplicate of the *in-progress* bucket stamped at its close → ahead by less than one cadence

Both are "ahead by less than a cadence". **No time threshold distinguishes them.**

## What actually distinguishes them — measured

```
20:00  open=4295.53  close=4302.50   <- spurious, stamped at bucket close
16:00  open=4295.53  close=4300.35   <- genuine, in-progress bucket
12:00  open=4290.22  close=4295.55
```

**`open[20:00] === open[16:00]`** — identical opens. The spurious row re-describes a bucket a
correct row already covers. Confirmed as redundant by observation: after deleting it, the
surviving `16:00` row's volume rose 49,373 → **52,708** as the bucket progressed. **No data was
lost.**

So the detectable signature is: **two consecutive rows for the same symbol+timeframe whose `open`
values are identical** (the next bucket's open should equal the previous bucket's close, not its
open).

## Proposed guard

A `contract-probe.cjs` invariant:

> **INV-NO-DUPLICATE-BUCKET** — for each symbol+timeframe, no adjacent pair of bars may share an
> identical `open`. A genuine next bar's open equals the prior bar's **close**; an equal **open**
> means the same period was written twice under different labels.

This is:
- **provider-agnostic** — it needs no timeframe table and no cadence knowledge
- **self-checking** — it can only fire on genuinely duplicated periods
- **immune to the boundary race** that forced `LEAD_FOR` to `cadence × 2`, because it does not
  compare against wall-clock time at all

It would **not** have replaced #832's writer guard — that stopped the row being written in the
first place, which is the right place to stop it. This is the **detection** half: the writer guard
is per-provider logic that can regress or be bypassed by a new writer path (exactly what the
`import-jobs.service.ts` gap was in #832), while a data invariant catches the outcome regardless of
which code path produced it.
