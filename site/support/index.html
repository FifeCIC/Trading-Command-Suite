Support the project

Trading Command Suite is free, open source, and built by one person. If it is useful to you, this page is how you can help it keep existing.

What your support actually pays for

My time. That is the whole of it — no servers to run, no data to license, no staff. I am a solo parent of four with limited working hours, and the honest truth is that I need the work to earn something or it cannot continue at this pace.

I would rather say that plainly than build a paywall quietly and pretend it was always the plan.

What stays free, permanently

Everything that decides whether a trade happens stays free, forever. Scoring, gating, protocol configuration, bots, positions, exits, risk controls, and the decision trail that explains an order. Those are not features to sell back to you — they are the part that has to be trustworthy.

Charging for risk controls would be indefensible. It is written here so that a future version of me cannot quietly forget it.

What may become paid, and what that means

The Lab — the advanced backtesting suite, with highly visual side-by-side A/B comparison of two configurations over the same bars — is intended as a paid tier for people who want a serious tool and would rather pay for it than be handed a shallow one.

And then it becomes free for everyone. When the income I have received after tax — from every source, the project included — reaches one million pounds, the Lab stops being paid and becomes free to all.

That is a commitment, not a slogan: it is recorded in the project's own planning documents with the date it was made.

Before you give me anything

This software is not financial advice, and it is not configured to profit by default. No strategy it ships with has been shown to be profitable. It is pre-release.

The most valuable thing you can do costs nothing: use a demo account and tell me what confused you. The second most valuable is reading the disclaimer before you connect an account.

If you would rather not pay anything

That is completely fine, and the software does not degrade because of it. Starring the repository, filing a clear bug report, or telling one person who might find it useful is worth more than a small donation.
